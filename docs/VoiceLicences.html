Voice licences and provenance

Voice models have separate terms from the plugin and speech engine. Check the model publisher's licence, required notices and model card for the version you distribute. Training-data and fine-tune-base records provide additional context; a permissive weight/dataset label does not describe every recording or recorded person's rights.

Qwen3-TTS models and preset voices

The catalog uses Qwen3-TTS CustomVoice GGUF models and a shared codec. The official checkpoints and the Serveurperso GGUF repository advertise Apache-2.0. Nine preset timbres are embedded in the model, not separate voice downloads. The technical report describes training scale, languages and stages, but not a complete recording-permission inventory for these timbres. Keep the required redistribution notices; see Licensing.

The lessac dataset licence grants research-only use and excludes commercial use of those materials. A permissive dataset used for fine-tuning does not establish permission to use the recorded base. Check the exact voice publisher's terms and recorded fine-tune-base terms before redistribution; dataset terms do not automatically determine the terms of every derived weight file.

Dataset labels also do not establish every weight publisher's terms. Preserve the exact card, model revision/hash and required credits. LJ Speech's public-domain recording claim is useful evidence; it is not a waiver of Piper/espeak/ONNX notices or a statement that the whole speech stack is obligation-free.

Redistributing a voice

For each voice you distribute, retain its model version/hash, source URL, model card and required notices. Check the engine/build, runtime libraries, preprocessing and dictionaries, and any reference recordings separately. Review the terms again when changing a model or voice.
